TURRET RECOGNITION SYSTEM — 64 TO 85 UNITS AT ~50 PX
Practical art-direction, UX and implementation specification
For the owner's 5×3 merge tower-defense board
Version: 2026-11-05

========================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE DECISION — WHAT I WOULD SHIP
========================================================================

Keep the existing polished, colorful rotating heads. Do NOT redraw all 64–85 turrets or paint every turret an entirely different color. Instead, build a two-level identification system:

(A) PERMANENT IDENTITY (consistent everywhere, even when a player changes deck):
    - Each turret receives one canonical emblem/glyph, one canonical accent color,
      one recognizable main silhouette, and one short name.
    - Identical ID appears on its collection card, deck card, info panel, and board.
    - Art is a skin of the turret ID; the emblem/color is the readable interface layer.

(B) MATCH-SPECIFIC DIFFERENTIATION (only 5 playable types in a normal deck):
    - Use the canonical identity by default; optionally add a uniquely colored
      small outer 'deck key' ring/marker for each of the five active turret types.
    - DO NOT recolor the whole head dynamically: it harms recognition of the
      original artwork and makes the same turret change identity across matches.
    - Highlight actual eligible merge targets instead of requiring players to
      visually search for duplicates during a drag.

P0: Add five-way deck identity colors + matching deck-bar keys; fix star badge;
    add tap-to-identify and drag-to-merge target highlighting.
P1: Add unique central/near-center emblems for all 64 heads; improve scale and
    suppress clutter; teach the five active units at match entry.
P2: Refine silhouettes/heads only for the genuinely ambiguous pairs and test
    with first-time players on a real phone.

TARGET EXPERIENCE:
Within the first minute, a new player can distinguish five equipped turret
TYPES; later, they learn the broader catalog by stable icon and name. Expecting
instant visual memorization of 85 previously unseen units is not realistic.
Design the system so nobody needs to memorize all 85 to play effectively.

========================================================================
1. DIAGNOSIS OF THE ATTACHED SCREENSHOT
========================================================================

The pictured match has a 5×3 grey industrial tray, shared grey circular plates,
mostly similarly round and similarly sized turret heads, a yellow star-number
badge at the upper-right of every cell, role/condition icons, several gold
selection frames, link lines, projectiles, bright beam VFX, and a highly
textured enemy lane. Different turrets are visually rich at collection-view
size but converge into the same pattern at phone-board size: 'dark grey ring
+ small colored parts + barrel/core'. Important identity details compete with
stars, role indicators, energy trails and board art.

Likely sources of confusion, in priority order:
1. Too little type-specific information is carried by a large, simple shape.
2. Colors are distributed among small details instead of one stable ID zone.
3. Supports and attackers often share the same circular silhouette.
4. The same dominant shared base grey takes up much of each visual.
5. Star badge, buffs and active VFX cover the silhouette/glyph.
6. Selection/connection decoration makes already busy cells busier.
7. No always-easy bridge from a board unit to its deck-card identity.

Do not confuse 'more detailed icon' with 'more identifiable icon'. At 50 px,
recognition is achieved by fewer, larger, distinct landmarks.

========================================================================
2. THE 50-PIXEL CELL CONTRACT (DESIGN AT ACTUAL RENDERED SIZE)
========================================================================

All pixel dimensions below are recommendations measured at FINAL on-screen
rendered CSS/device-independent game-pixel size of approximately 50×50. If the
game uses a smaller logical canvas and scales it, multiply consistently by the
actual render scale. Review screenshots at 1× final size, not zoomed in.

Cell footprint:                   50×50
Center of turret:                 (25,25)
Usable head envelope:             36–39 px across (attack barrel may extend
                                  to ~43 px; leave a 2–3 px safety inset)
Shared base plate:                <=39–41 px diameter; preferably behind head
Canonical ID mark/emblem:         14–18 px visual width, ~16–20 px height
                                  where it can sit without obscuring a weapon
Emblem negative-space holes:      >=2 px at final display
Critical emblem stroke:          >=2 px; 1 px only for nonessential detailing
Head silhouette/readable part:    >=6 px characteristic landmark
Type color outer arc/key:         2.5–3 px thickness; ~26–34 px ring diameter,
                                  visible 180–270 degrees, interrupted where
                                  appropriate by star and weapon
Star badge:                      13–15 px across; upper-right, consistent
Star numeral:                    10–12 px bold bitmap/sans, high contrast
Optional role glyph:             7–9 px only in inspection/deck view; avoid
                                  permanent second tiny badge on every cell
Tile spacing:                    maintain >=2 px clear separation of visual
                                  shapes between neighboring tiles

LAYER ORDER (bottom to top):
0 board socket → 1 subtle neutral base → 2 type accent ring/key → 3 turret
head → 4 turret ID emblem (if not already integral to the head) → 5 star
badge → 6 status overlay → 7 selection/merge glow → 8 transient attack VFX.
Do not render the ID emblem as a constantly rotating top layer if it needs to
remain upright. Prefer a fixed upper cap or a billboarded overlay keyed to the
turret's screen position. For directional heads, keep the functional weapon
rotating and the readable ID mark stationary.

RULES:
- 1 primary focal point per turret, not three competing glows.
- On ordinary play, a 50 px cell should expose ID + star only. On selection,
  show ability/name/neighbor links in an adjacent inspection area.
- Do not spend >25% of a turret's visual interior on decorative bolts and
  trim; reserve that space for emblem, face, weapon, or unique functional core.
- Reduce background brightness and desaturate industrial metal. Preserve
  cyan enemy lane/core and actionable feedback, not constant bright trim.
- A unit must remain identifiable when effects are disabled and in grayscale.

3. WHAT TO BORROW — AND WHAT NOT TO COPY
========================================================================

RANDOM DICE:
Its useful design lesson is compact, repeated visual grammar: a small set of
chosen dice, simple recognizable type visuals, and an extremely conspicuous
pip/dot count. Standard merge uses same type and same dot count, with special
exceptions. Its readability comes from a coherent system, not from giving
every die an unrestricted neon hue. For your game, a type glyph is analogous
to a die's central visual identity; the numeric star badge is analogous to its
pip count. Keep these two readings visually separate.

DESTINY CHILD: DEFENSE WAR:
The practical model is the five-unit lineup plus leader, random summon from
that lineup, merging compatible units at equal star level (with special-unit
exceptions where applicable), and mana power-up from the deck bar. This means
players mostly need to distinguish five actively equipped types during a
match, not the entire collection. Therefore optimize five-at-once contrast,
learnability and selection feedback, then improve the 64–85-unit collection.

DO NOT copy either game's art assets or imply there is a known official color
algorithm. The palette/overlay system below is a design proposal for this game.

References reviewed:
- Random Dice mechanics (historical page; explicitly warns its version is old):
  https://random-dice.fandom.com/wiki/Dice_Mechanics
- Random Dice starter guide (2026-10-28):
  https://randomdicehub.com/guides/random-dice-beginner-guide/
- DCDW overview / deck mechanics:
  https://www.mobygames.com/game/176430/destiny-child-defense-war/

  https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/destiny-child-defense-war/dcdw-beginner-guide-en.html

4. THE RECOMMENDED IDENTITY SYSTEM: 4 DISTINCT CHANNELS
========================================================================

CHANNEL 1 — CANONICAL GLYPH (strongest long-term identifier)
Each turret gets exactly one stable pictogram: a unique symbolic contour,
centered on the head where possible, or on an upper stationary cap. Make it
recognizable as a 16 px filled silhouette. Avoid microscopic illustration.
Examples: Cannon = muzzle ring; Dice Gun = die face; Frost = snowflake;
Cloner = twin blocks; Chain Link = interlocked links; Overlord = forked bolt.
Never reuse an emblem across two turrets, even if they share role/element.

CHANNEL 2 — CANONICAL ACCENT (stable in menu, card, battle, tutorials)
Assign a default hue to each named turret. A palette of 85 individually
'unique' colors is NOT reliably discriminable at 50 px. Use 10–12 distinct
color families, plus glyphs and silhouettes. Color is a secondary clue.
Keep original art hues; place the stable color on the same clearly visible
2.5–3 px ring/key on every turret and the deck card border.

CHANNEL 3 — SHAPE/ROLE GRAMMAR (predicts what the turret does)
Attack = directional projection (barrel, blade, muzzle, bow, missile pod).
Sub-attack = short/paired/projected tool, still clearly directional.
Support = radial/symmetric field device; NO fake weapon barrel.
Control = symmetric field shape or a recognizable trap/projector, visually
separate from gun types.
Special/merge utility = transformation glyph, box, prism, clock, DNA, crown;
no weapon-like barrel unless it genuinely attacks.
Role grammar predicts FUNCTION; the unique glyph identifies the exact TYPE.

CHANNEL 4 — STAR LEVEL (independent variable)
Stars 1–7 must have one reserved high-contrast badge in the SAME position on
all turrets. The appearance of the head, glyph, accent and tier should not
change because the unit moves from 1★ to 7★ unless the upgrade itself is a
specific visual evolution. Use a numeral, not 1–7 tiny stars scattered around
its circumference. Keep tier/rarity on collection card, not confused with star.

Rarity vs identity vs role:
- Rarity (white / blue / purple / gold / red): shown on collection/deck card
  frame and details, NOT as the primary always-on turret ID rim.
- Type: emblem + canonical color + silhouette.
- Role: shape grammar, optionally a small UI icon in info/deck view.
- Star: fixed top-right badge 1–7.
- Buff/debuff: temporary icon or overlay, visually subordinate.

5. COLOR SYSTEM: FIXED ID + OPTIONAL FIVE-DECK KEY
========================================================================

5A. CANONICAL COLOR PALETTE (12 example anchor hues)
These are STARTING VALUES for testing against the dark board, not a promise
that every pair is perceptually distinct or color-blind safe.

Coral red       #F05B55       Burnt orange   #F58B30
Amber gold      #E6B338       Lime           #A6C83A
Leaf green      #46B86A       Teal           #26B7A9
Cyan            #47C9E9       Royal blue     #4D83E9
Violet          #9770E9       Fuchsia        #D46AC9
Warm white      #E7D7B8       Copper         #BB8061

Paired border outline: dark #18202A, or near-black #11151C. Avoid letting
cyan vs teal, red vs orange, purple vs fuchsia be the only distinction.
Always test with color-vision deficiency simulation and grayscale.

5B. RECOMMENDED 'DECK KEY' ALGORITHM
The board contains just five active unit TYPES. It is reasonable to assign
five visually separated KEY COLORS to those types for the duration of a match.
However, these should color a thin ADDITIONAL key segment/ring, not the entire
turret head. Keep canonical color/glow/art on the turret itself.

An initial test set of five highly separated deck-key hues:
1 blue       #4D90F0
2 amber      #FFC34A
3 cyan-teal  #33C6C0
4 coral      #F06A71
5 violet     #B48AF3

These are not guaranteed color-blind-distinct. Add five DISTINCT shapes to
the keys (e.g. dot, square, triangle, chevron, diamond) on deck cards and
optionally as tiny ring cutouts on the board. Better: use the turret's full
unique glyph as the shape cue. Never assume hue alone is sufficient.

Map TYPE_ID → deckSlotIndex → matchKeyColor ONCE at match setup, and keep it
constant for this match, across summons/merges/reconnections/replays. Do not
reassign slots when units are upgraded or die. Prefer the actual sorted deck
slot order (1–5) because it is predictable and already visible in the HUD.

Example: Five chosen IDs are [Cannon, Radar, Frost, Star Cannon, Mystery Box].
Cannon gets deck key blue, Radar amber, Frost cyan-teal, Star Cannon coral,
Mystery Box violet. Their original orange/teal/ice/gold/purple artwork stays.
A matching colored 3 px corner tick appears on each corresponding deck card.

CAUTION: A player may find a gold Star Cannon marked coral surprising. Hence:
- P0: prototype runtime keys in an optional 'Quick Read' mode and test them.
- If this mapping makes learning worse, use fixed canonical colors only, and
  instead maximize distinction among five types using a neutral outline,
  five distinct corner shapes, and selection highlights.
- Never switch palette on the same player between moments of one match.
- For PvP opponent board, use their own five fixed mapping slots consistently.

5C. IF USING PER-DECK COLOR OPTIMIZATION (ADVANCED)
A later algorithm may select colors from 8–10 candidates by maximizing the
minimum perceptual distance across the active five keys in a color-vision-
deficiency-aware perceptual space (e.g. OKLab/OKLCH simulation). BUT do not
let arbitrary re-optimization recolor one unit from match to match without
an anchored label. For a launch-ready game, the fixed five-slot palette with
shape backup is simpler and more reliable. Do not ship a mathematically
complex solution before player tests demonstrate value.

7. RIM, DECK CARD, STAR BADGE, LABEL AND FEEDBACK
========================================================================

7A. RIM
Base structural metal remains dark, neutral and stable. Attach a colored
'identity arc' INSIDE or just above that base ring where it is not occluded.
This can be an overlay sprite or programmatically drawn geometry. Recommended
3 px thick, 65–80% circumference, with one deliberate gap near a barrel.
The current shared base should not be the only large visible contour.
When a turret rotates its head, do not rotate the entire ID ring.

7B. DECK BAR MATCHING
Each of five deck cards shows: same turret art thumbnail, same emblem (12–16
px), same type/ring color, same short name in non-battle menus, and rarity
ONLY as a slim outer card accent. Match identity to board exactly. Color is
not substituted by tier. For active-selection, dim unrelated cards and
highlight all matching placed turrets.

7C. STAR DISPLAY — REPLACE THE AMBIGUOUS TINY GOLD STAR SHAPE
On the screenshot each '3' floats on a yellow star-like badge. Make one fixed
high-contrast dark-inside badge with a large numeric '3' (and optional tiny
star cutout) at top-right. It must never be hidden under a firing effect.
Suggested: dark charcoal hexagon 14×14; 1.5 px pale-gold border; bright ivory
bold numeral (1–7), 11 px cap height; 1 px dark shadow for legibility.
Keep all seven stars the same badge footprint. A 1–7 segmented ring would
compete with the type-colored ring, so avoid it in normal play. Don't use 7
miniature dots or seven mini stars; phone players cannot count them quickly.
For 7-star apex, add a SMALL crown gleam outside badge only if legible.

7D. LABEL/IDENTIFY UX (PRIORITIZED)
Tap a turret (single tap, no drag) → bottom/near-board info strip immediately:
'FROST  •  3★  •  SLOW' plus large 28–36 px head icon, rarity and one-line
function. Placement should not block the player's drag origin. Strip persists
until next tap or brief timeout (suggest 2–3 seconds only if auto-dismiss
works with gameplay; allow closing).

Long press (hold 350–450 ms with <6 px movement) → expanded inspect card:
short name, canonical emblem, hero/pilot portrait, ability in one sentence,
star, upgrade tier, compatible merges. Long press should NOT trigger mid-drag.

On deploy/summon → briefly pulse identity ring and show 0.7–1.0 second name
chip above the tile ONLY for the first 2–3 summons of each type in the first
few matches or if 'show unit names' accessibility toggle is on. For experienced
players, no repeated labels clogging the board.

On first use/FTUE → show five deck types as a 5-card mini legend with each
unit's colored key + emblem + 1-line role. Then ask player to identify one
board unit, tap for description, and perform one legal merge. Do not force
all 64 tutorials. After the player demonstrates competence, fade aids.

Optional settings: Quick Read (emblems/keys always emphasized), larger star
badges, reduced VFX, show names on summon, and high-contrast mode. These must
not change gameplay mechanics.

7E. MERGE DRAG FEEDBACK — MOST IMPORTANT GAMEPLAY FIX
When pointer is down and intent to drag has passed threshold:
- Lift origin 2–3 px / show small source ID emblem near touch location.
- Mark all legal same-TYPE + same-STAR destinations with a clear thin PULSING
  border (e.g. 3 px bright mint #75F0B9 + a small white center pip), or a
  contrast-safe alternate based on background.
- Dim all invalid occupied cells ~25–40%, but do not reduce board awareness.
- On hovering an eligible target, grow its border to 4 px and create a short
  connective arc from source to target; avoid permanent link clutter.
- On hovering invalid cell, show neutral cross/slash and never imply legality.
- After release, remove preview within 1 animation frame; on success, one
  strong merge burst only (<=250–350 ms), and show new ID immediately.
- Special merge units (Mimic, Copycat, etc.) use actual authoritative game
  rules; don't hard-code same ID/same star if exceptions exist.
- On cancellation, restore all cells to normal.

Tap deck card → outline every matching TYPE on the board for ~0.8 s, regardless
of star, while keeping the star badge visible. This is a useful low-effort
'where are my X units?' action.

7F. VISUAL EFFECTS BUDGET
Reduce passive glow, perpetual lightning and VFX over icon centers. Put active
projectiles ABOVE the unit layer but avoid covering center glyph longer than
~100 ms. Behind-barrel lightning should not linger on adjacent heads.
For selection, use ONE outline style; for linked neighbors, use thin 1.5–2 px
lines with endpoint dots, shown only while selected or truly meaningful.
If multiple statuses happen, combine icons in inspect strip rather than
stacking 4+ pictograms on the 50 px tile.

8. SILHOUETTE AND ROLE RULES FOR ALL FUTURE ART
========================================================================

OFFENSIVE:
Main form 'circle + forward protrusion'. Directional muzzle occupies roughly
20–35% of head diameter; one characteristic muzzle shape per weapon. Cannon
= one fat tube; Gatling = barrel cluster; Scatter = fan; Sniper = needle;
Railgun = two rails; Laser = single aperture; Mech Wolf = muzzle + claws.
Do not let every attacker become the same one-cylinder-right silhouette.

SUB-OFFENSIVE:
Directional weapon is short (e.g. trap, splash, status projector), with a
separate distinctive source/core. Visual cue says 'secondary damage/utility'.

SUPPORT:
Strictly no fake barrel. Symmetric layout with central glyph and 3–4 radial
modules. Mana Core = crystal; Crit Antenna = four spike target; Chiller = fan;
Amplifier = speaker+coils (radial); Shield Gen = hex shield; Radar = sweeping
rings; Cooldown Horn = symmetric horn/gear. Do not point a support 'dish' to
RIGHT merely to match attackers; directional orientation would mislead.

CONTROL:
Traps, clamps, portals, shields and cold-field motifs; an enclosing ring or
opposed arms is more useful than one more cannon muzzle. Keep the core glyph
prominent; show field radius only on selection.

SPECIAL / MERGE UTILITY:
Strong icon-like center (question box, crown, two cores, DNA, mirror, prism,
clock, mimic), minimal hard-to-read small hardware. This is the category with
most potential to read like Random Dice while preserving the art around it.

All support a 1-color and grayscale silhouette test. Distinguish by OUTER
shape first, CENTER glyph second, COLOR third, glow last.

10. CODE-ONLY IMPLEMENTATION — NO ART REDRAW REQUIRED
========================================================================

SPRINT 0 — MEASURE / INSTRUMENT (0.5–1 day)
1. Capture screenshots from an actual iPhone landscape at final gameplay
   scale (including low-brightness mode and saturated VFX moment).
2. Record cell hitbox, physical visible size and real sprite occlusion.
3. Add debug overlay: turretId, starLevel, deckSlot; disabled in release.
4. Track wrong-type merge attempts, successful target time, inspection opens,
   wrong upgrades, and tap-to-identify use (no personal data needed).

SPRINT 1 — UI CORRECTIONS (1–3 days)
1. Implement new 14×14 fixed numeric star badge layer and contrast outline.
2. On selection, show typeName + role + stars in 1-line strip.
3. On deck card tap, highlight all cells with matching turretId.
4. Disable continuous selection glow and most passive link lines by default.
5. Ensure deck card frame/key uses same ID mapping as board.

SPRINT 2 — MERGE PREVIEW (1–3 days)
1. On pointerDown record source turretId, starLevel and coordinates.
2. Enter drag after 6–8 px travel (or game-tuned slop); tap otherwise.
3. Ask the game RULE ENGINE for legalTargets(source, boardState), including
   special-case merge mechanics. UI must never separately reimplement logic.
4. Add highlight to legal cells, dim invalid cells; don't animate every cell.
5. On pointerMove update candidate target; on pointerUp validate again.
6. On merge success show single compact burst; on cancel clear preview.
7. Check touch priority: scrolling/inspection, drag start, long press.

SPRINT 3 — TYPE COLOR OVERLAYS (1–2 days)
1. Add ID_KEY color arc on a draw layer between base/head or above head edge.
2. Register canonical colors; add 5-key accessibility mode to test.
3. Match deck cards. Persist mapping for match/replay deterministically.
4. Build color-blind and grayscale QA screenshots; add glyph/shape backup.

EXAMPLE DATA CONTRACT (pseudocode; not a specific engine API):

TURRETS = {
  4:  { id:4, name:'Scatter',     role:'attack',  glyph:'scatter_fan',
        color:'#F58B30', rarity:'common', silhouette:'fan5' },
  19: { id:19,name:'Crit Antenna',role:'support', glyph:'target4',
        color:'#F05B55', rarity:'rare', silhouette:'radial4' },
  26: { id:26,name:'Chiller',     role:'support', glyph:'snow_fan',
        color:'#47C9E9', rarity:'rare', silhouette:'fan4' }
};

function startMatch(deckIds) {
  const keyColors = ['#4D90F0','#FFC34A','#33C6C0','#F06A71','#B48AF3'];
  return new Map(deckIds.map((turretId, slot) => [turretId, {
    slot, keyColor:keyColors[slot], glyph:TURRETS[turretId].glyph
  }]));
}

function beginDrag(source, board, rules) {
  const legal = rules.getLegalMergeTargets(source, board);
  // Pass legal to renderer; do not infer it from art or color.
  renderer.setMergePreview({sourceCell:source.cell, legalCells:legal});
}

function renderCell(cell, matchIdentity) {
  drawNeutralSocket(cell);
  drawTypeArc(matchIdentity.get(cell.turretId).keyColor);
  drawExistingHead(cell.headSprite, cell.heading);
  drawGlyphOverlay(cell.turretId);  // optional until glyph art arrives
  drawStarBadge(cell.starLevel);
  drawStatusMinimally(cell.status);
}

Consider using a monochrome glyph placeholder from available vector/UI shapes
for only a few turrets during P0, then replace with curated icons at P1.
Do not rely on generic emoji: platform rendering is inconsistent and too
complex for 16 px. Avoid canvas text glyphs if font loading differs on iOS.

ENGINEERING EDGE CASES:
- Glyph stays screen-upright when the head rotates; ID still readable.
- Capture touches correctly near top-right star badge and tile boundary.
- Same turret type in multiple cells always has same icon and ring color.
- 7★ cap and locked units show no fake merge target.
- Special fusion rules use game engine authoritative decisions.
- Cloner/Mimic effects that visually copy others need explicit ID indication:
  original ID and current copied form should not be confused.
- Transforming units: identify CURRENT gameplay type during action; offer
  original/base name in detail panel if useful.
- Opponent board must not be mistaken for player's merge targets.
- Freeze/poison/slow overlays must not replace the underlying ID glyph.
- Device performance: batch static arcs in texture atlas; update glow only
  on interaction. Keep overdraw low on mobile browsers.
- Touch is not hover: every affordance must work with touch alone.

========================================================================
12. VALIDATION / A-B TEST: DEFINE 'INSTANTLY IDENTIFIABLE'
========================================================================

Test at 1× display scale on the target iPhone, one hand, during real combat.
Recruit at least 8–12 novice testers for formative sessions; larger sample
for a release A/B test. Mix bright and dim environments. Do not coach them
on each icon before the cold-read task.

TEST A — 5-TYPE MATCH IDENTIFICATION
Give five deck cards, show 15 mixed units for 5 seconds, then ask:
'Tap every Frost', 'Which is Radar?', 'Which unit was just summoned?'
Measure percent correct, median decision time, and error clusters.
Suggested acceptance goal after a short introduction: >=90% correct within
~1 second/type selection; set actual release threshold from baseline.

TEST B — LEGAL MERGE
In a 5×3 mixed board, ask 'Merge the eligible Scatter pair'.
Record time-to-correct-drop and rejected drops. Goal: >=95% successful on
first attempt after one explanation and >=30% lower median time than old UI.

TEST C — STAR READABILITY
Flash 15 tiles at 1× size. Ask number on randomly picked tile and compare
old yellow star badge against new numeric badge. Goal >=95% star read accuracy.

TEST D — LONG-TERM TRANSFER
After playing with one deck, give 5 different units; do players use glyph,
shape and deck strip rather than memorizing a transient color mapping?
Test canonical-only against five-key mode. If five-key colors harm transfer
or cause a gold turret to feel inconsistent, turn the option off by default.

TEST E — ACCESSIBILITY / EFFECT LOAD
Repeat in grayscale/color-vision simulation, lower brightness, and on a
busy VFX scene. At 50 px the ID must survive with color disabled. Record
largest confusion pairs and prioritize only those for art repair.

DO NOT claim these numerical goals are proven industry standards; they are
proposed pass/fail criteria for this project's experiments.

A RECOMMENDED DEFAULT MOCKUP, EXPRESSED IN WORDS:
Every cell = neutral industrial square, existing turret head, a SINGLE bold
identity arc on the outer ring, a screen-upright single-function emblem on
the turret body, and ONE readable '3' star badge at upper-right. No permanent
labels, no 4-neighbor beams unless selected. Five deck cards beneath use
identical ID accents + icon. Tap reveals full name. Drag illuminates only
legal targets. This preserves the original game's colorful art while making
its controls approachable to new users.

14. DECISION CHECKLIST / DEFINITION OF DONE
========================================================================

[ ] Every ID from 1 to 64 has canonical glyph, color, role and name.
[ ] Newly added IDs 65–85 require these properties before acceptance.
[ ] Same type always looks the same in deck and board.
[ ] Five equipped types visually separate at final phone scale.
[ ] Shared gray bases no longer dominate the identity layer.
[ ] No support unit advertises a misleading gun barrel.
[ ] Main glyph survives rotation, grayscale and active combat VFX.
[ ] 1★ through 7★ readable without counting mini symbols.
[ ] Legal merge targets are clearly identified on drag; special rules work.
[ ] Tap identifies any turret in one gesture.
[ ] New player tutorial only teaches the five active deck types initially.
[ ] Name labels are available, but not permanently crowding the board.
[ ] Testers are measurably faster and make fewer identification mistakes.

FINAL RECOMMENDATION
Ship merge highlighting + tap identification + a legible star badge first.
Then introduce stable unique glyphs and a clear deck/board identity key.
Reserve art redraws for evidence-backed failure cases. A fresh player does
not need to know 85 detailed sci-fi turrets: the board needs to give that
player an immediate, consistent way to identify the five that matter now.
