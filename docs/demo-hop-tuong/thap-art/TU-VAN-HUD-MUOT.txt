TURRET MERGE — PORTRAIT MOBILE UI/UX REDESIGN

Senior Art Director’s Production Specification | 390 × 844 reference viewport | 5 November 2026

SOURCE AND CONFIDENCE This review is based on the six supplied TURRET
MERGE screenshots (left to right: menu/deck picker, Cannon detail,
battle, battle with Sniper info, online lobby, boss introduction). DCDW
and Random Dice are design references, not sources of reusable assets.
No side-by-side DCDW screenshot or instrumented DCDW build was supplied.
Accordingly, any statement about specific DCDW pixel sizes, animation
timings, exact component counts, or screen flows would be speculative.
‘Reference-informed’ below means a broadly recognizable genre convention
or visual impression, NOT a verified DCDW implementation. All
measurements and motion values below are proposed TURRET MERGE targets,
not measurements of DCDW.

EXECUTIVE DIRECTION Current problem: almost every available surface is
treated as a decorated, information-bearing control. Dark embossed
plates, tiny labels, intricate borders, multiple accent colors, and
repeated card frames compete at the same priority. The result is not
primarily a lack of polish; it is a lack of editorial selection. Make
the game board and turret silhouettes the stars. Keep a deliberately
premium industrial sci-fi identity, but reserve ornament for navigation
anchors, rare rewards, and primary calls to action.

Three non-negotiables: 1. One dominant decision per screen; one dominant
action per screen. 2. Keep full game information accessible, but not
simultaneously visible. 3. All visual geometry, gradients, frames,
icons, shadows, glows, and effects are pre-rendered AI-generated PNG
sprites; runtime code may position, crop, scale, tint by alpha/opacity
where appropriate, rotate, animate transforms, and draw dynamic
text/numbers only. No runtime vector rectangles, CSS gradients, borders,
shadows, or procedural shapes.

1.  WHY THE DCDW-STYLE EXPERIENCE FEELS SMOOTHER

EVIDENCE LEVELS KNOWN FROM YOUR SCREENSHOTS: Your menu combines logo,
player identity, pick count, three sorting/filter controls, search,
category tabs, preset deck cards, a large turret roster, mode choices,
and two bottom actions. Your Cannon page displays character art, an
equipment portrait, stat strip, skill copy, an extended test-range
preview, and a bottom remove button. Battle shows a compact 5×3 board
with a large amount of unused vertical dark field; an information
overlay consumes lower board-adjacent space. Lobby uses a central modal
over an already dense menu. Boss intro shows three small framed boss
cards with substantial empty space. REFERENCE-INFORMED, NOT VERIFIED
DCDW FACTS: A polished hero/collection experience tends to emphasize
expressive full-bleed character art, an immediately readable primary
action, calmer visual grouping, and selectively revealed details.
Merge-defense battle UIs typically protect board visibility and place
economy/summon controls at a stable thumb-friendly edge. These are
design patterns, not claims about exact DCDW UI. GENERAL BEST PRACTICE /
PROPOSED TURRET MERGE TARGETS:

Visual hierarchy: target 3 attention levels. Level 1 = screen’s central
choice (selected turret, active board, or READY). Level 2 = supporting
actions (deck slots, summon cost, navigation). Level 3 = metadata
(rarity, stats, descriptions). At a glance, viewers should identify
Level 1 in under 1 second. Use contrast and scale before decorative
borders.

Visible element budget: menu landing 5–7 major groups; collection/deck
builder 4–6 groups; detail 4–5 groups; battle 4 fixed HUD zones plus
board; lobby 3–4 groups; boss intro 2–3 groups. A ‘group’ is a
perceptual cluster, not an individual card or digit. This is a review
target, not a DCDW count.

Spacing and safe area: design in logical 390×844 units; top inset 44,
bottom gesture inset 34 as provisional iPhone-style values; adapt
dynamically to real device insets. Outer horizontal gutter 20–24; gap
between major zones 16–24; within components 8–12; touch targets >=44×44
(prefer 48–56). Leave 8–12 between adjacent tappable objects. Never
place critical text beneath notches or gesture areas. On Android,
recalculate rather than hard-code insets.

Typography: one condensed display face for large headers and numbers,
one clean sans for labels/body. Proposed sizes in logical px: 28–32
screen title, 22–26 hero name, 18–20 key numbers, 15–16 primary
controls, 13–14 secondary labels, 11–12 tertiary metadata only. Minimum
readable UI text target 12–13 at 1×; never shrink core battle data to
9px to fit ornament. Weights: bold for hierarchy, medium for labels,
regular for descriptions. Restrict ALL CAPS to short tags (e.g., EPIC,
READY).

Color discipline: near-black graphite #10141B and slate #222B35 as
bases; warm brass #D9A64A for primary action/reward; icy cyan #4EDCE8
for selected/interactive/energy; off-white #F0F2F4 for text; muted gray
#8996A4 for tertiary copy; red #ED5967 only for danger/HP; purple for
rarity/unique resource only. Limit each screen to 1 dominant accent + 1
contextual accent; the battle itself already supplies colorful turret
imagery. Avoid using cyan, green, orange, purple, and gold all as
equivalent button highlights.

Frames and plates: three reusable families: (A) light neutral recessed
plate for passive content, (B) medium brass mechanical frame for cards,
(C) rare luminous premium frame for active choice/legendary moment.
Reduce border detail by ~50% on repeated elements. One outer border is
enough; avoid stacked frames inside frames inside panels. Keep
decorative corner bolts on hero/primary plates, not every statistic.

Depth: pre-render soft contact shadow and narrow rim highlights into
PNGs; background at lowest contrast, content plates mid contrast,
selected items highest contrast. Simulate elevation through 2–4px PNG
sprite offset and pre-rendered shadow states. Use dark translucent PNG
scrims to isolate modals. Do not blur the entire game screen merely to
suggest premium depth.

Motion: continuity is a polish multiplier. Prefer 180–300ms navigation,
90–130ms press response, and restrained overshoot on selected items.
Keep static text stable; animate image layers, opacity and translation
rather than constantly pulsing everything. A successful merge can be
energetic; the whole HUD should not be.

Icon density: a pictogram should replace text only if its meaning is
learned and unmistakable. Never place more than 2–3 small metadata
glyphs in a card’s default state. Put advanced tags in detail view.
Standardize icon bounding boxes (20/24/32px) and stroke/silhouette
weight.

Progressive disclosure: roster card -> selection preview -> detail
sheet; battle turret tap -> short inspect popover -> optional expanded
details; boss preview -> one signature mechanic per boss -> optional
‘How to counter’. Information must be discoverable, not erased.

Full-bleed character art: use a large turret pilot or hero illustration
that bleeds beyond a simple rectangular portrait, with its focal
face/gun kept clear of text. Place title and CTA on a calm lower plate
or dark pre-rendered fade. This is a proposed way to capture
character-forward appeal; it is NOT a claim that every DCDW screen uses
full-bleed art.

3.  REDESIGNED INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

HOME: profile, hero art, current mode, PLAY, Deck/Collection/Settings
navigation. DECK BUILDER: five-slot loadout + simple 3-column turret
grid + filters; tap turret to select; tap Details for full page;
separate Presets drawer. UNIT DETAIL: full-bleed pilot + turret render +
one-line skill + 2×2 essentials + sticky Add/Remove. BATTLE: one top
status bar + board + quick inspect + bottom action tray. LOBBY:
standalone room invitation, two player rows, READY, exit. BOSS PREVIEW:
brief pre-match overlay or dedicated intro sequence, never a permanently
complex layer.

FLOW: HOME -> DECK -> (tap card -> preview sheet -> DETAILS optional) ->
HOME/PLAY -> SOLO battle or ONLINE lobby -> boss preview -> battle. HOME
-> Collection uses same roster component as Deck but without loadout
selection. Persist mode and deck state.

4.  MOTION AND FEEL SPECIFICATION

EASING TOKENS (proposed, not DCDW measurements) FAST_OUT =
cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1) SOFT_IN_OUT = cubic-bezier(0.4, 0,
0.2, 1) SPRING_LITE = cubic-bezier(0.18, 1.15, 0.32, 1) (subtle
overshoot) EXIT = cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1) Prefer 60fps; no
unnecessary layout recalculation; animate image transforms/opacity and
numeric text only. Respect reduced-motion preferences (replace
scale/slide with 120ms crossfade).

Navigation: Home -> Deck: outgoing art 180ms fade, incoming backdrop
220ms fade, panel 260ms slide +12px -> 0, stagger card row 20ms each
capped 100ms. Detail opens from tapped card with 240ms image-scale
continuity if technically feasible; otherwise 220ms crossfade and 16px
upward translation. Back reverses in 180–220ms. Lobby entrance 280ms
with background fade and invite plate rising 20px. Boss intro 300ms art
reveal; 100ms stagger between boss tiles.

Button press: on touch down, button image scales to 0.965 in 75ms,
switch to pre-rendered pressed sprite, release 120ms SPRING_LITE to 1.0.
Primary CTA adds 90ms localized gold flash sprite, never a whole-screen
white flash. Disabled button stays quiet; invalid action uses 2× 4px
horizontal shake over 180ms and optional haptic tick.

Roster card: on touch down scale 0.98/80ms; on select, cyan/brass
selected overlay PNG alpha 0->1 in 140ms; card rises 2px over 160ms;
tiny check badge enters at 0.85->1 scale 160ms; selected slot receives a
180ms warm rim sweep PNG. Do not use permanent fast pulsing. On deselect
120ms fade, no explosion. Desktop hover is not a mobile primary state;
use pressed/focused/selected states.

Deck add: selected turret portrait flies as a small image duplicate from
card center to target slot in 260–320ms FAST_OUT, with destination slot
pulse 140ms. If motion is disabled, immediate slot swap + 120ms
highlight. Replacement must make old and new card identities obvious.

Numbers: currency gain text interpolates in 220–350ms, integer step
easing out; critical damage number 160ms upward drift + 120ms fade; wave
number swaps via 180ms vertical roll. Do not animate every small stat in
the HUD at once. If a numerical value changes repeatedly, coalesce
updates to avoid unreadable rolling.

Battle summon: 100ms button press, 180ms summoning energy PNG, 220ms
turret image pop 0.75->1.05->1 with 2px settle; cap total ~400ms. Merge:
90ms convergence, 140ms impact sprite, 220ms resulting turret scale
settle; total 450ms, while retaining immediate input response.
Board-full feedback 180ms shake and no huge message.

Quick inspect: 150ms slide up 8px + fade; switching selected turret
crossfades content 100ms with stable panel geometry; dismissal 120ms.
Expanded sheet 260ms up from bottom with 180ms scrim. Don’t force timed

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA / ART-DIRECTOR SIGN-OFF - From Home, a returning
player identifies PLAY in <=1 second and starts a known mode with <=2
deliberate taps. - In Deck Builder, the five selected turrets and
remaining slots are immediately legible; a player can replace a slot
without reading instructions. - Every turret card remains recognizable
at native phone resolution; selected state is unmistakable without
relying solely on color. - During battle, the board, incoming enemies,
HP, currency, summon availability, and commander readiness are readable
without opening an overlay. - Quick inspect never obscures a tappable
board cell; extended stats remain one tap away. - Lobby room code,
share, partner status, and READY are visually unambiguous. - Boss intro
is readable and never interrupts live combat without a deliberate
pause. - All visuals originate in generated PNG assets; runtime renders
only text/numbers and image transforms/crops. No code-drawn decorative
geometry. - At 390×844, minimum touch targets >=44px and critical
labels >=13px unless validated by device testing.

FINAL ART DIRECTION The solution is not to make the metallic art flatter
or less distinctive. It is to give that art fewer, more important places
to shine. Use one beautiful hero image instead of six competing
portraits; one confident primary button instead of several
equal-priority buttons; one coherent battle status rail instead of
multiple banners; one quick-inspect line instead of a miniature
encyclopedia. Make the industrial frame support the turret, not compete
with it. That is the difference between ‘more decoration’ and ‘more
polish.’
