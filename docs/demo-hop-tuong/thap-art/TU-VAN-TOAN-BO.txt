TURRET MERGE — COMPLETE MOBILE UI/UX ART-DIRECTION AUDIT
English | Target viewport: 390 × 844 logical px | 5 November 2026
Based on four supplied composite screenshot sheets. Layout dimensions below are proposed specifications, not measurements extracted from original source files. Verify against actual device safe insets and production captures.

EXECUTIVE VERDICT
Visual identity 7/10; hierarchy 5/10; moment-to-moment battle readability 5/10; cohesion 6/10; perceived polish 6/10. Subjective expert assessment, not measured user research. This is already recognizable as a graphite/brass/cyan anime sci-fi game. Its biggest problem is not low-quality illustration but cumulative noise: nearly every control has a heavy bevel, gold trim, multiple outlines, micro-labels and a glow. The result feels like a collection of attractive panels rather than one carefully orchestrated interface. Keep the art direction; simplify its grammar. Reference DCDW, Random Dice and Rush Royale for decision clarity, not for copying their art or claiming every specific implementation is identical across versions.

NON-NEGOTIABLE IMPLEMENTATION CONSTRAINT
All visible ornaments, cards, icons, frames, shadows, glows, badges, progress fills and effects must be ChatGPT-made raster PNG art. Runtime code may place, scale, clip, mask/crop and composite PNGs, and render text. No runtime drawing of vector rectangles, strokes, gradients, circles or procedural effects. For scalable elements, request PNG 9-slice art or fixed-width variants; a nine-slice is still raster PNG placement. Use RGBA transparency for production; flat #FF00FF is the art-generation/keying background only. Beware magenta spill/anti-aliasing when extracting alpha. Maintain a common atlas, scale policy and safe padding.

A. GLOBAL ART DIRECTION AND DESIGN SYSTEM
1. Hierarchy: level 1 = one primary action or threat; level 2 = decision controls; level 3 = metadata; level 4 = ornament. Currently level 3/4 frequently compete with level 1. Give every screen ONE dominant accent. On Home: gold Play; Deck: cyan selected slots plus gold Play; Battle: cyan/mana and red boss warnings; Result: gold victory or restrained red defeat.
2. Palette: backgrounds near charcoal #10151C / #1B222B; steel #303943 / #47505A; aged brass #A47B3A / #D7AE60; cyan #38C9DD as selection/energy, not general decoration; alert red #E45555; readable text #F4F5F7, secondary #ADB7C1. These are proposed UI tokens, not mandatory replacements for existing sprite colors. Rarity must retain COMMON neutral white, RARE blue, EPIC purple, LEGENDARY gold, MYTHIC red; use rarity mostly in a small consistent chip/rim, not a full glowing border on every element. Pilot illustration colors remain expressive.
3. Material family: A = hero/primary plate (rare); B = standard interactive plate (quiet); C = inset information strip (almost flat); D = modal surface (single strong header). Do not use A everywhere. Use one edge thickness family: 2–3 px apparent rim on regular controls, 4–5 px only on hero/primary. One shadow direction (down 3–5 px), one lighting direction (upper-left). Avoid stacking cyan outer glow + white rim + brass lip + drop shadow on the same small card.
4. Spacing: viewport horizontal safe content x=16..374 (358 wide); inner gap 8 or 12; section gap 16 or 24; major modal padding 16; minimum touch area 44×44, preferred 48×48; 8 px minimum between independent tap targets. Respect OS top/bottom insets; coordinates assume full 390×844 content and must be inset-adjusted when necessary.
5. Typography: render text in code, not baked into PNG. One family with 3 weights. Main page heading 20–24 px; section heading 15–17; primary buttons 17–20; body 13–15; metadata 11–12; micro labels only when optional, 10–11. Avoid letter-spaced all caps for body copy. Contrast target >=4.5:1 for normal essential text, >=3:1 for large text and meaningful non-text indicators; test on device. Never use gold or cyan micro-text on noisy art without a quiet plate behind it.
6. Icon language: icons should use a unified chunky silhouette, shared optical stroke, and one 24×24 or 28×28 drawing box. Colored rarity, resource, status and action semantics must not be conflated. Rarity = rarity badge; merge level = numeric/star badge; mana = purple gem with number; selection = cyan mechanical check; warning = red hazard. Add text or shape redundancy for color-vision accessibility.
7. Motion: transitions 180–240 ms; button press 80–120 ms; card selection 140–180 ms; merge drag pickup 90–120 ms; drop snap 140–200 ms. Use PNG frame sequences for spark/burst/energy. Respect reduced-motion option. Sound/haptics should confirm pickup, valid target and completed merge, never replace visual feedback. Animations must not delay input or obscure active board state.
8. Screen density: the default view should show only the information required for its next decision. Details live in a secondary sheet. Avoid a separate frame around every line of explanatory text. DCDW-like clarity is largely about keeping the board as the hero and making unit selection feel fast; Random Dice-like clarity comes from distinct merge values and predictable board feedback. These are design lessons, not assertions about exact current competitor UI pixels.

C. BATTLE INFORMATION PRIORITY AND READABILITY
At a glance (under 1 second), the player should locate: 1) what is approaching / boss time, 2) where a merge is valid, 3) available mana and summon cost, 4) current lives, 5) current merge rank of each turret. Rarity is primarily a roster/deck concept; during battle, rank, targeting and active skill matter more. Current screenshot does not consistently achieve this because rank badges, turret glows, rarity-like colors, muzzle effects and board outlines overlap.

MERGE RANK: one badge position, e.g. upper-right 18–22 px circular chip, numeral 14–16 px bold; optional small stars only if they remain legible. Never show both a huge star motif and separate tiny number with unclear relationship. Rank 7 needs the same placement as rank 1. The rank badge should have an opaque dark backplate PNG.
VALID TARGETS: on drag start, raise source sprite ~6 px and scale 1.06, show a 2-frame lift shadow PNG; eligible same-rank compatible targets receive cyan 2 px pulse frame PNG; ineligible targets dim slightly with a PNG overlay, never red unless action is prohibited. Show a ghost head preview on target and clear result feedback. Avoid highlighting every square if only a subset is valid. Invalid drop returns to origin within 140–180 ms without losing input; successful merge fires a 6–8 frame PNG burst and settles in 180–240 ms. If game rules require identical turret type rather than only rank, target highlighting must encode that exact rule.
THREATS: enemy health and boss attack tells must have greater visual priority than decorative turret glows. Red = imminent danger; amber = countdown; cyan = player resource/selection. Provide boss countdown as 'BOSS IN 8' not an ambiguous small number. Distinguish enemy path from board with luminance and subtle edge, not dozens of tiny cyan arrows. A high-threat enemy needs a distinct silhouette/outline or status glyph; do not rely solely on red color.
MANA: 18–20 px number in one fixed bottom-left region; purple gem icon 24–28; summon cost adjacent to summon button. If cost increases, animate only the changed digits/plate. Keep the upgrade control separate from summon; 'FULL' needs a precise semantic definition and distinct disabled state. Pause/speed controls must be >=44×44.
RARITY: deck cards and roster get small consistent color chips; in-battle turret head should not be ringed with rarity glow if it compromises rank readability. If rarity impacts combat decisions, expose it in the tap inspector.
TEST: five first-time players should identify current mana, lives, next boss countdown and the merge rank of a chosen turret in <=2 seconds each from a paused screenshot. At least 90% of valid target highlights should be correctly understood in an interactive test. These are proposed acceptance targets, not observed outcomes.

D. PORTRAIT SYSTEM: CROP, EYE LINE, SAFE AREAS
The uploaded art shows actual eye/forehead cuts and a suspicious line crossing portraits. Solve with a consistent image-generation composition, normalized anchor coordinates, and clipping discipline. Do not ask a model to 'make the head smaller' without a crop specification.

MASTER PORTRAIT: create square 1024×1024 source with full hairstyle/hat, shoulders and signature weapon visible; top empty headroom 10–12% (102–123 px); eye line at 34–40% of image height (348–410 px); face horizontal center within 42–58% of width; 10–12% side padding around hair; no critical face feature within outer 12% edge. Allow signature weapons to extend into lower corners, not across eyes. Generate alternate crop compositions if a unit has unusually tall horns/hats. Store face anchor and top-of-hair anchor metadata per portrait; do not rely on one crop offset for all 85.

ROSTER CARD: art window 104×90 within 114 px card. Eyes should land at ~32–42 px below top of art window; hair top >=8 px below top; no frame bars inside the inner 88×74 face-safe window. Pilot art is scaled to cover only when both eyes and full hair remain visible; otherwise use contain or a custom bust variant. Frame corners outside inner face-safe window. Name below in 12–13 px, single line.
SELECTED DECK SLOT: outer 66×78; portrait window about 58×58, top margin 6; eyes y=22–29 relative to window; head/hair top >=6; overlay border <=3 px inside and decorative top bar outside face window. Small check at slot upper-right, never over an eye. If the original portrait cannot fit, request a dedicated slot bust with more headroom.
BOTTOM BATTLE TRAY: outer card ~61×78; portrait window 52×52; eyes y=19–25 relative to window; badge at outer lower-right; cost text on bottom strip, not on face. Keep faces large enough to recognize in <1 second.
TAP INSPECT PORTRAIT: 44×44 image; eyes y=16–19; no frame ornament across forehead. Use a dedicated tight face crop anchored to eye line, not a downscaled full bust.
DETAIL HERO: 390-wide page, artwork area x=16..374 y=74..376; face eyes approximately y=176–214 in viewport (34–46% down artwork); full hair/hat with >=20 px headroom, shoulders/weapon below; avoid putting a giant turret medallion over eyes or mouth. Hero crop should be an independent high-res composition if necessary.
COMMANDER HERO: same geometry as detail hero; commander crest at lower-right of torso area, not on face.
BOSS PREVIEW PORTRAIT: full silhouette in 240×210 central art window with >=12 px inset; eye line only relevant for humanoid bosses; prioritize readable attack silhouette.

FRAME LAYER RULE: render portrait PNG first; render decorative frame PNG second with transparent interior; render rarity/check/rank chips last in reserved corner zones. Never draw a horizontal metal bar across the face window. Use a diagnostic magenta/green checker under transparent interior during QA (diagnostic only, not shipped), and inspect pixel alpha. If a hairline seam persists, check atlas bleed, UV sampling, half-pixel placement, PNG premultiplication and clipping bounds. Use 2–4 px extruded atlas padding, integer-aligned final pixel edges where practical, consistent texture filtering and alpha mode. Fix the source/layer issue rather than painting a concealing stripe.

FINAL ART-DIRECTION DECISION
Do NOT restart the art pipeline or discard the distinctive anime/metal look. The premium upgrade is subtractive: 30–40% less decorative chrome on secondary surfaces, bigger and consistently framed faces, one clear primary action, reliable merge feedback, and readable tactical data. The art already sells fantasy; the interface now needs to sell confidence and speed.
